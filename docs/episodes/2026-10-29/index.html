Odysseys2: did the browser agent actually finish?

Story transcript

Our lead today is Odysseys two, a browser-agent benchmark with a very reasonable demand: show your work, including the actual file you produced. Researchers at Carnegie Mellon and Browser Use built a hundred tasks from real user requests. Their strongest tested agent finished fewer than half perfectly. For developers, that's a useful antidote to impressive-looking demos. The tasks and rubrics are available, though automated judging and a changing live web still make comparisons imperfect.

Redox rings: less copying between system services

Story transcript

Down in operating-system land, Redox has published the results of its ring-buffer project. Applications and system services can exchange requests through shared memory, with less copying along the way. The team also built an asynchronous driver executor and demonstrated storage support. It's plumbing, but the useful kind. The work finished in September; this week's report explains it. Their benchmark gains still need testing against messier, more realistic workloads before we generalize.

Systems & infrastructure#13 ↗

Async Rust: an explicit state for being done

Story transcript

For the Rust crowd, here's a tiny missing word with surprisingly large consequences: done. Yosh Wuyts argues that async Rust needs a clearer way to represent a future that already returned its value. Polling it again can currently panic or cause other trouble. An explicit finished state could simplify coordination and help embedded use cases. This is a design argument, not a shipped language change. Compatibility is the awkward part.

Why picked
Article published October 29; current specialist discussion inspected. Community observation: 18 points, 3 comments at 2026-10-29T17:54:35.677976+00:00.

Small specialist discussion; a single observation of interest, not measured acceleration, adoption or proof of global obscurity.

Document Parsing Protocol: write down the ambiguous choices

Story transcript

Speaking of documents, what counts as a table when the page is just carefully aligned text? The Document Parsing Protocol tries to make those judgment calls explicit. Its working draft covers tables, forms, marginal notes and other awkward details, with examples and rules for uncertainty. That could make parser evaluations much easier to compare. It's an early proposal, not an established standard. The interesting test is whether different implementations can follow it consistently.
